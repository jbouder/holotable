A theme is a named set of colors. Each comes in a light and a dark color
scheme, and you choose the two separately in **Settings → Appearance**, where
the [theme gallery](/guide/settings/#appearance) previews each one, or from
the command palette (type "Theme"). The choice is kept in this browser and
applies before the page draws. An operator picks the theme a new browser
starts with (`THEME_DEFAULT_PALETTE`). A browser that never chose one follows
that default, including when the operator changes it.

Every theme is held to the same bar: AA contrast for all text and AAA for body
text, visible borders and focus, and status colors and chart series that stay
apart for readers with a color-vision deficiency. The rules and how they are
measured are on [Theming](/architecture/theming/#quality-bar).

## How to choose

- **Holotable** if you have no reason to change. It is the default.
- **Graphite** if the blue feels cold, or a wall of panels should show only the
  data in color.
- **Fjord** for long sessions, when you want the page calm without losing
  contrast.
- **Paper** for a projector, a printout, or anyone who reads a dashboard like a
  report.
- **High contrast** if AA is not enough for you, or the room is bright.

## Holotable

Lifted slate surfaces, soft corners and a quieter series palette. Cards sit a
little above the page on a soft shadow, corners are rounded, and the eight
chart series are calm enough to share a dashboard without shouting, while
staying apart under every color-vision deficiency the checks cover. In light,
the warning is a burnt amber rather than brown. Its statuses and series are
the reference the other themes are measured against. If you want the square,
flat look the default used to have, choose Graphite, Fjord or High
contrast, or load your own theme with `radius` `0px` and `shadow` `none`
(see [Custom themes](/admin/themes/)).

## Graphite

A true neutral. The surfaces are warm grays with almost no color, so a chart
is the only colorful thing on the page. The accent is violet, chosen because
it is far from every status hue: a link or a selected tab is never mistaken
for a warning. Statuses are quiet so they sit in a neutral
page. The chart palette leads with the accent, then blue, teal and
amber.

## Fjord

Cool and calm without being low in contrast. The surfaces are tinted
blue-green and the accent is a sea teal. Secondary text is a shade warmer
than the surfaces, so it reads as ink rather than fog. The chart palette comes
from the sea side of the color wheel, with an amber and a coral series for
contrast. Amber comes second, so the first two series never share a hue
family. Success leans yellow-green so it stays well apart from the teal
accent. The light scheme is a pale mist, not white.

## Paper

Light first. Ivory surfaces, near-black ink and a deep blue accent, with chart
colors inked slightly darker than the other themes so thin lines hold on a
light page. It is the one theme with rounded corners. Its dark scheme is a
warm charcoal, not an inversion of the light one.

## High contrast

For people who need more than AA. Every text is at least 7:1 against what it
sits on, and body text is at least 12:1. Borders are at least 4.5:1, and the
background and cards are nearly one color, so edges carry the structure
rather than shades. Badges, alerts and status tiles are solid fills instead
of tints. Chart series are spread across lightness as well as hue, the
grid is visible, and the marks behind a value (a stat's sparkline, a gauge's
threshold bands) are drawn stronger. Its bar is stricter than the other themes', and it is
described on [Theming](/architecture/theming/#quality-bar).

## Your operating system's colors

If your operating system forces its own colors (Windows High Contrast, also
called contrast themes), it overrides every theme. Holotable keeps edges and
focus visible in that mode. It also redraws charts in the system's colors,
because the system cannot recolor a chart itself.
